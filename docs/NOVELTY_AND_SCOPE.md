# Initial novelty and scope audit

**Date:** 13 October 2026  
**Finding:** Global novelty and exclusive legal ownership are not established by this project.

## Nearby fields

1. Adaptive diffusion networks are an established research area. See Tiglea, Candido, and Silva, “Adaptive diffusion networks: An overview,” *Signal Processing* 223 (2024), 109570. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2024.109570
2. Nonlinear diffusion on networks and consensus dynamics have been studied. See Bonetto and Jardón Kojakhmetov, *Networks and Heterogeneous Media* 19(3) (2024), 1344–1380. https://doi.org/10.3934/nhm.2024058 ; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04442
3. Adaptive rewiring and diffusion on weighted networks are also established topics. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-62204-7

## What is and is not claimed

Graph Laplacians, diffusion, consensus, dynamic weights, bounded state variables, Lyapunov functions, and numerical ODE integration are standard mathematical ingredients. They are not claimed as inventions of this project.

GMT v0.1 gives one explicit coupled formulation with bounded saturating gradient response, first-order edge-memory relaxation, a proof bundle, tests, and reproducible simulations. Whether this precise system is novel relative to every publication, thesis, patent, technical report, and unpublished manuscript has not been established.

## Required next steps for a credible novelty claim

1. Search MathSciNet/Mathematical Reviews, zbMATH, arXiv, Crossref, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, theses, patents, and relevant physics/engineering databases.
2. Search alternate descriptions: gradient-dependent conductance, adaptive edge mobility, state-dependent graph Laplacian, conductance learning, edge-state memory, and gradient-square relaxation.
3. Compare equations after nondimensionalization and transformations, not only title keywords.
4. Ask independent experts in nonlinear dynamics, network science, and mathematical physics to identify nearest prior art.
5. State the distinction relative to the closest work and derive a prediction or theorem that actually separates the model from baselines.

No finite search can prove that no similar idea exists anywhere. A defensible contribution may be a new theorem, a carefully delimited generalization, or a useful application rather than a wholly new branch of physics.

Numerical tests validate implementation behavior for tested cases; they do not prove global novelty or empirical truth.
